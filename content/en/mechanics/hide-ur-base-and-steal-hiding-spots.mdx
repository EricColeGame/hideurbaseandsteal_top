## Core Mechanics: Camouflage, Hitboxes, and Audio Profiles

Survival sandbox mechanics rely on a triangle of detection factors: line of sight, physical collision boxes, and audio cues. If any of these three reveals your location, your hidden stronghold becomes an open target. Players often treat defense as simply stacking thick walls, but smart defenders build deceptive geometry.

To succeed, you must understand how third-person camera angles give attackers visual advantages around corners. Building tight, zigzag corridors prevents raiders from zooming out their perspective to peek into your inner sanctum.

| Detection Element | In-Game Mechanic | Tactical Countermeasure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Line of Sight (LoS)** | Raycasting from player models | Visual baffles, false closets, low lighting |
| **Hitbox Collision** | Overlapping character mesh barriers | Hollow floorboards, crawlspaces, corner clipping |
| **Audio Signatures** | Footsteps, tool impacts, inventory sounds | Crouch-walking, decoy noise generators, carpeted pathways |
| **Third-Person Peeking** | Free-cam clearance over lower obstacles | Low ceilings, decorative overhangs, tight 90-degree chicanes |

Community reports highlight that crouching under decorative props or blending your character's avatar color with common interior textures (like concrete or dark timber) dramatically cuts visual spotting distance.

## Defensive Base Architecture: Layouts That Confuse Raiders

Designing an unraidable layout is less about raw durability and more about psychological misdirection. If you structure your base like a straightforward dungeon, raiders will systematically clear every corner with explosives and weapons fire. Instead, your goal is to make invaders feel they have already looted your primary cache.

| Room Type | Primary Function | Deception Factor | Recommended Trap Pairings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Entryway Airlock** | Restrict group breaches | Low | Springboards, fall traps |
| **Bait Vault** | Display worthless, unlocked loot | Extremely High | Tripwire alarms, fake barricades |
| **Dead-End Labyrinth** | Drain enemy stamina and munitions | Moderate | Blind drop-offs, visual smoke emitters |
| **Internal Safe Room** | True storage and survival bunker | High | Concealed hatch doors, silent keypads |

### The "Onion Layer" Defense Strategy

1. **Outer Shell:** Made of cheap, weathered materials that make your compound look abandoned or already looted.
2. **Mid Layer:** Contains obvious corridors, unlocked wooden chests with junk items, and intentionally broken walls to sell the illusion.
3. **Core Shell:** Built behind an irregular rock wall or recessed decorative asset, completely unlit to prevent player silhouettes from showing through seams.

Implementing this layout ensures that when enemies try to scout the building, your actual presence remains completely undetected.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does it mean to hide ur base and steal hiding spots?
This tactic combines concealed base building with active flanking. Instead of hunkering down in a static bunker, defenders slip out through hidden tunnels during an attack to seize the high ground, cover, or extraction zones that raiders assumed were safe.

### How do I prevent other players from using third-person peeking against my hiding spots?
Keep ceiling clearances low (no more than 2 to 2.5 character heights) and avoid flat, uninterrupted hallways. Adding archways, low-hanging chandeliers, and 90-degree turns blocks the virtual camera from rotating around obstacles, forcing players to expose their avatars to clear rooms.

### What is the most common mistake made when trying to hide ur base and steal hiding spots?
The biggest mistake is having only one entry and exit point. If an attacker breaches your singular door, you are trapped in your own hiding spot. Every concealed safe room must feature an emergency escape hatch leading to another section of the map.

### Can raiders hear me shifting or looking around inside a hidden wall?
In most survival engines, turning your character's view or toggling inventory items produces faint audio cues. Crouch-walking completely silences movement, but opening containers or drawing heavy gear can reveal your location to players wearing headsets nearby. Keep actions to a minimum until the threat moves past.
